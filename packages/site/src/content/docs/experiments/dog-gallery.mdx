Every dog sprite sheet we have, measured and lined up on the same ground, ready
for the elevator demos.

The sheets come from a pixel-art pack and don't quite agree with each other.
Frames are 64 to 85 pixels wide, the dogs inside them are different sizes, and
some leave a gap of up to 8 pixels under their paws. So rather than trusting the
frame, each sheet is measured, and every dog stands on its **anchor**: the
middle of the dog, just under its paws as it stands still in the sheet's first
frame. Sitting and sleeping dogs can curl their tails below their paws, so the
other frames don't count.

The measuring happens ahead of time, not in your browser, in a package of its
own, `packages/sprite-prep`. It saves what it finds in the site's
`src/assets/dogs/measurements.json`, and packs every sheet into one file,
`dogs.kennel`. The pack's licence lets us use the sheets but not hand them
out, so the kennel scrambles them: it isn't an image anyone can open or save,
and git doesn't keep the PNGs at all. A page loads the kennel and the numbers,
and nothing else.

Switch _Stand on_ to _Frame_ to see what happens when you trust the frame
instead.

## Lining up animations

Every sitting, sleeping and lying sheet starts with the dog standing still, but
not always in the same spot as its standing sheet, and a tail or a stretched-out
leg can make the dog look wider than it is. So each sheet's first frame is
matched against the standing dog's head and body (not its legs, which move the
most), and its anchor goes under the same part of the dog. The walking sheets
are matched too, though they're drawn a little differently. _Lined up_ says how
well each sheet matched, and the _Rest pose_ guide draws the standing dog behind
it, so you can see for yourself.

Sitting, sleeping and lying are round trips: the dog gets down, stays down for a
few frames, then gets up again. Those middle frames are its **hold**, which a
waiting dog will loop for as long as it waits. _Frames_ plays a sheet the way
the pack's GIFs do (_All_), the way a waiting dog will (_Wait_), or just its
hold, to check it loops without a jump (_Hold_). Open _Whole sheet_ to see each
frame's number, with the hold underlined in green.

## Adding a sheet

1. Copy the pack's `<breed>/<colour>/spritesheet/<Animation>-Sheet.png` into
   `packages/sprite-prep/sheets/`, putting the dog's name in front, e.g.
   `husky-gray-Sitting-Sheet.png`. Git ignores that folder, so it stays on your
   machine.
2. Add it to `packages/site/src/sprites/dogCatalog.ts` with its frame count,
   which is how many frames are in the pack's GIF of it.
3. Run `bun run site:sprites` to put it in the kennel and measure it. It takes
   the PNGs in `packages/sprite-prep/sheets` and keeps the rest of the kennel as
   it was, so you only need the PNGs you're adding or replacing. Unit tests
   check that the kennel holds every sheet in the catalog, each divides into
   whole frames, and `measurements.json` is up to date, so run it again
   whenever a sheet, its frame count, its `facing` or the catalog's
   `restAnimation` changes.
4. If the dog gets down and up again, give it a `hold`: the first and last
   frames it stays down for, counting from 0.
5. If a dog still looks a bit off, give its sheet a `nudge`. Holds and nudges
   are added as the page loads, so they don't need measuring again.
